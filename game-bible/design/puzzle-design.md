# Puzzle Design — Resonance System

## First chamber

Three adjustable resonators (working number). The player discovers calibration clues through nearby machinery, damaged terminals, and sound. Each resonator changes visual and audible feedback. Correct configuration triggers an ancient maintenance response and ship reconstruction.

## Rules

- No perfect pitch requirement: provide visible waveforms, symbols, patterns, and accessible feedback.
- Correct solutions should be logically deducible.
- Different sites should vary puzzle structure: gravity, electromagnetism, timing, alignment, acoustics, signal routing.
- Frequency fragments form a larger recognizable motif without every dungeon being a musical keypad.
- Later reveal may show a subtle difference between original containment sequences and manipulated variants.

## Story integration

The siren signal manipulates contemporary machines; resonance signals control ancient machinery; the protagonist's inherited memories can explain uncanny recognition without solving puzzles automatically.

## Technical exploration

Web Audio API for tones; React Three Fiber for physical resonance visualizations; Zustand for puzzle state. Prototype with three discrete frequency bands before investing in rich sound synthesis.

## Beyond machinery

Frequency puzzles should gradually introduce *interpretation*: a pattern first used to operate a resonator might later help initiate contact with a being, or reveal that a familiar cultural melody is a message. Avoid turning every encounter into the same input-a-sequence puzzle. See `design/musical-language.md`.
