# Decision Log

Dates reflect documentation date; individual ideas developed in conversation leading up to 2026-10-23.

## D-001 — Siren navigation signal
**Status:** Established direction  
**Decision:** The spheres emit structured frequencies that nearby spacecraft interpret as legitimate navigational instructions.  
**Why:** Adapts siren folklore into a technological mystery and connects directly to the resonance mechanic.  
**Open:** Physical explanation for cross-species/machine compatibility.

## D-002 — Transport failure, not attack
**Status:** Established direction  
**Decision:** Sphere transit is ancient and deteriorating. It transports the ship to a remote facility but accidentally tears it apart.  
**Why:** Gives the technology original purpose, plausible environmental damage, and more nuanced entity behavior.

## D-003 — Deliberate destination
**Status:** Established direction  
**Decision:** The imprisoned intelligence routes the crew to a particular facility because it needs the protagonist there. The ship damage was not its intended result.  
**Why:** Makes the opening purposeful without requiring the entity to control every accident.

## D-004 — Ship reconstruction
**Status:** Established direction  
**Decision:** Solving the first resonance puzzle triggers ancient machinery that rebuilds the vessel and installs a transit interface.  
**Why:** Strong dungeon reward, provides a hub, enables handcrafted interstellar progression.  
**Open:** Whether reconstruction is an authentic maintenance routine, corrupted protocol, or both.

## D-005 — Protagonist authorization
**Status:** Established direction  
**Decision:** Ancient transit requires the protagonist aboard, passively; other crew can pilot conventionally.  
**Why:** Connects story identity to mechanics and supports the ship theft scenario.  
**Open:** What is actually recognized.

## D-006 — Morally complex ancient conflict
**Status:** Tentative, favored exploration  
**Decision:** Do not assume the imprisoned being is evil. Consider an ancient war with another intelligence, both having defensible aims and harmful methods.  
**Why:** Adds moral ambiguity and reframes the apparent antagonist.  
**Open:** What the war was about, who built the prison, what catastrophe is imminent.

## D-007 — Heroic relative
**Status:** Tentative  
**Decision:** Explore a protagonist living in the shadow of a famous heroic relative, as an original thematic homage to Mass Effect.  
**Why:** Personal stakes and identity conflict independent of ancient destiny.  
**Open:** Relationship, nature of heroism, how it intersects with inherited memories.

## D-008 — Solo prototype first
**Status:** Established direction  
**Decision:** Build a small React Three Fiber prototype personally before considering a team or studio.  
**Why:** Validate the distinctive mechanic and creative vision with manageable scope.

## D-009 — Music as language and emotional throughline
**Status:** Favored creative direction; implementation and lore tentative  
**Decision:** Expand frequency sequences beyond environmental puzzles into a learnable way of recognizing and communicating with other beings. Let musical motifs accrue narrative and emotional meaning, including in the score.  
**Why:** Unifies gameplay, atmosphere, character relationships, and the ancient mystery; creates a distinctive identity for the game.  
**Guardrails:** Not instant translation, not a universal emotional code, and no pitch/rhythm gatekeeping.  
**Open:** Origin, grammar, sensory forms, player interaction model, and what the central theme ultimately means. See `design/musical-language.md`.

## D-010 — Production direction
**Status:** Established planning target, subject to playtesting and resources  
**Decision:** Prioritize a standalone first game, with an aspirational ~10-hour scope, but build a polished 15–20-minute prototype first at approximately 5–10 hours per week. Use the prototype to test the concept and potentially attract collaborators.  
**Why:** Preserve a complete first story while keeping near-term work manageable. A possible trilogy is a future option, not a production commitment.
