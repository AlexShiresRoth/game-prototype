# Story Outline — Working Acts

**All later acts are tentative.** The opening's transit failure and reconstruction are stronger directions than the precise endgame.

## Act I — The Siren

A freelance crew approaches a recently appeared artificial sphere to investigate missing ships. A structured frequency signal masquerades as valid navigation corrections. The vessel is drawn into the sphere and transported to a specific remote facility selected by the imprisoned intelligence. Ancient transit machinery is decaying; the transfer shreds the ship unintentionally. The protagonist wakes among wreckage, searches for survivors, hears fragments of the signal, and accesses mechanisms that seem to recognize them. A resonance puzzle activates an ancient maintenance protocol, reconstructing and altering the ship with a network transit device. The crew can now travel—but not directly home. The device requires the protagonist's passive presence as an authorization key.

## Act II — The Journey

The crew visits other systems through a limited set of ancient routes, encountering alien civilizations, prospective companions, and other people drawn to the Objects. Different puzzle worlds yield frequency fragments and clues about the network. The ship becomes a lived-in hub. The crew learns of an approaching catastrophe and races to return to loved ones.

## Act III — The Apparent Enemy

A recurring saboteur blocks activations, steals research, or damages mechanisms. They seem to prevent the crew from getting home. In truth they may be a former operator who activated a lock, saw disastrous consequences, and now tries to prevent further catastrophe. Their tactics remain morally questionable. They may themselves be influenced by the opposing ancient intelligence.

## Act IV — The Prison and the War

The worlds are components of a containment network. Living operators are needed to manipulate certain locks. The entity within the prison has guided different operators with tailored promises; some succeeded, some failed, some resisted. The protagonist carries inherited memories from someone in the original civilization, potentially involved with the network. A newer possibility reframes the imprisoned intelligence and its opponent as morally complex participants in an ancient war. Neither side's account can be taken at face value.

## Act V — The Sequence

Fragments form a larger frequency pattern. Distinguishing genuine maintenance instructions from manipulated sequences becomes critical. Potential resolutions include repairing, opening, or reconfiguring the network. The real origin of the conflict and the consequences of either being's victory remain open. The protagonist must decide who to trust and how to protect the crew and home systems.

## Character arc

The protagonist begins as an ordinary person with a real identity, possibly overshadowed by a legendary relative. They become valuable to the network because of inherited ancient memories, but must reject the idea that ancestry or legacy determines their choices.

## Player knowledge versus hidden knowledge

- **Early:** The sphere's signal hijacked navigation; the transfer damaged the ship; ancient machinery can be operated by resonance.
- **After first dungeon:** The facility can reconstruct and modify the ship; it offers routes, but not home; protagonist-specific authorization is required.
- **Midgame:** Other operators exist; the apparent enemy's actions may have protective effects; the sequences control more than transportation.
- **Later:** The network contains locks; ancient intelligences have manipulated the conflict's narrative; the protagonist's inherited memories are evidence, not necessarily truth.
